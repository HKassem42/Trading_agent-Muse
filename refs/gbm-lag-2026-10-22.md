## What was tested

Kass's hypothesis: every model so far saw a single-row **snapshot**.
Add **trajectory**: for each of the 11 focus features (Volume, OBV,
Force Index, Elder Force Index, Historical Volatility, Mass Index,
Bollinger BandWidth, Connors RSI, Accumulative Swing Index,
Ultimate Oscillator, Choppiness Index), its values 1h, 2h and 3h
ago and its changes over 1h and 3h — plus raw close-return lags
(ret_1h, ret_2h, ret_3h, ret_6h, ret_12h, ret_24h; simple pct
returns). Labels are **unchanged** from the focus experiment
(next-candle return, ±0.3% fee band) — one variable at a time.

All lag columns are past-only by construction (positive shifts /
differences of positive shifts). Two independent causality proofs:
the tests pin hand-computed values and future-row invariance on
fixtures, and the build's setup phase rebuilt the frame on the
first 9,000 rows and matched the full-frame build **exactly**
(max diff 0.0) on sampled lag/delta/return columns.

Data: the same real BTC matrix (16,735 rows). Lag warmup dropped
the first **24 rows** (ret_24h is the longest memory), leaving
15,937 pre-duel rows (focus build: 15,961) and the same 792h duel
region.

Learner, hyperparameters, thresholds (0.40/0.40, pre-registered),

Reading: raw accuracy *rose* for every lag arm — in the wrong
direction. The lag models predict HOLD more often (69–71% vs
61–66%), drifting back toward the majority-class collapse, and on
balanced accuracy — the metric that punishes that drift — **every

< C 0.4086, G 0.4157 ≈ D 0.4163. Trajectory added columns, not
signal. No arm beats always-say-HOLD on accuracy; the best balanced
accuracy in either experiment remains the snapshot kitchen sink's
0.4163, against a 0.333 chance level — a whisper, unchanged.

### The flag on arm F's "pass"

Arm F clears the gate *formula* (aggregate −1,871 beats the
champion's −2,063; 2 of 3 windows won) with real activity — 40
round trips, trades in every window; it is not the silent-challenger
case. **It still lost $4,416.98 in a month while buy-and-hold made
+$717.54.** The pass exists only because the bar is a champion that
lost $4,485 on these windows. In absolute terms F is a losing
strategy that lost slightly less than the champion; it is
registered promoted=0 with this verdict in its reason, exactly
like the focus build's flagged passes.

### What the lags actually did to the money

- **Focus arms lost less.** E lost $3,548 less than its snapshot
  twin B; F lost $1,791 less than C, at comparable activity (59 vs
  66 and 40 vs 37 trips). Read carefully: the walk-forward says the
  underlying calls did **not** improve (balanced accuracy fell), so
  this is loss *reduction*, not edge — the lag models sat out some
  of the worst moments via their drift toward HOLD. Less bad is not
  a challenger.
- **The kitchen sink got worse.** G lost $6,107 against D's $3,178
  (aggregate −2,523 vs −1,604) at similar activity. Tripling the
  width of a signal-free feature set mostly added overfit surface.
- **SMOTE + lags is still SMOTE.** F is the least-bad focus arm on
  the duel and the worst focus arm on walk-forward balanced
  accuracy; the two gradings disagree, which is itself the signature
  of noise. The focus build's conclusion stands: forced activity
  converts to losses after fees; lags shrink the conversion rate
  without changing its sign.

## Permutation importance — did trajectory matter at all?

Measured on the best lag arm by OOF balanced accuracy (arm G, 186
columns; OOF scheme, 3 repeats — the wide arm's cost cap, vs 5 in
the focus build). Shares are % of total *positive* importance.

**By column kind:** current snapshots 40.7% · deltas 31.1% · lags
23.0% · return lags 5.1% — trajectory columns total **59.3%** of
positive importance. But trajectory columns are 126 of G's 186
columns (67.7% of the design), so per column they
**under-perform their representation**: the model spreads small
amounts of weight over many trajectory columns, and no individual
trajectory column separates from noise (the best, Volume lag1, is
+0.0032 ± 0.0022 — its error bar is most of its mean).

**The top of the table is volume-shaped, again:**

Current Volume is ~8.5× the #2 column — the same lone standout the
focus build measured (there: +0.0388, 48.1% of a much smaller
table). Summed across its variants (now + lag1 + d1), the Volume
family is 19.3% of arm G's positive importance; no other base
feature exceeds 3.1%. Among the return lags, the mid-horizon pair
leads (ret_6h #3, ret_3h #7), ret_24h is #25, and the shortest
horizons are at the bottom of the table (ret_12h #99, ret_1h #116,
ret_2h #139) — if trajectory held anything, it would be the
3–6h return window, at noise level. The single best trajectory
column is **Volume one hour ago**, i.e. the trajectory of the one
feature that already mattered as a snapshot.

## Verdicts in plain words

- **Arm E (lag focus, weights):** Lost $4,885, 1/3 windows, worse
  balanced accuracy than its snapshot twin. FAIL. Trajectory made
  the weighted model more cautious, not more right.
- **Arm F (lag focus, SMOTE):** The only lag arm to clear the gate
  formula — with real trades — while losing $4,417 and losing to
  buy-and-hold by over $5,100. A formula pass over a losing
  champion, flagged as such. Not a challenger.
- **Arm G (lag all-60, weights):** Lost $6,107, 1/3 windows — the
  worst lag arm and worse than its snapshot twin on every money
  metric. FAIL. Width without signal is just overfit surface.
- **Overall — did trajectory help? No.** On signal metrics every
  paired comparison is flat-to-worse; on money metrics the focus
  arms lost less only by leaning harder on HOLD, and the wide arm
  lost more. Kass's snapshot critique was fair and the test was
  clean — the answer is that the recent *path* of these indicators
  carries no more exploitable information than their current
  values, at least for next-hour direction on BTC. This is the
  fifth independent null on 1h BTC (TA stacker, PPO, HA-sequence,
  A-Z screen, GBM focus + lag) and the park holds. All three arms
  are registered in the lab (runs 25–27, promoted=0).
